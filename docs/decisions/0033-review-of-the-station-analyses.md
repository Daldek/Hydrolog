# 0033. Review of the station analyses: one name per concept, variant options, an offline batch, the reference period of normals, Mann–Kendall ties and the list of spells

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-13
- Amends: [ADR-0029](0029-station-analyses-for-obserwator.md), [ADR-0030](0030-the-batch-and-imgw-pib-half-years-in-frequency-commands.md), [ADR-0031](0031-regime-seasonality-harmonics-sens-slope-and-baseflow.md), [ADR-0032](0032-meteorological-statistics-of-a-station-the-group-meteo.md) (none of them released: AGI-4.1 lets an unreleased document keep its number)
- Builds on: the author's request of 2026-11-13 (check that the CLI and API of the new commands match what `develop` `48358a5` deploys, then resolve three methodological questions); plan `docs/plans/2026-11-13-station-analyses.md`; Nogat AGI-1.2, AGI-2.3, AGI-4.2, AGI-5.4, DEV-8.2, DEV-19.10, ERR §1

## Context

Steps S1 to S4 of the plan added 14 commands and extended 7 on four stacked branches. Each was checked against its own plan, not against the catalog `develop` deploys (CLI, MCP, the GUI's „Wszystkie polecenia”, `hydrolog-result/1`). The review compared both catalogs option by option (name, type, unit, default, `one_of`), ran every catalog example and read the result documents, notices, error reasons, the batch's envelope and the MCP annotations. Three questions were left open by S4: normals of a reference period other than the analysed years, Mann–Kendall's S on floating-point precipitation sums, and the length of the list of spells.

## Decision

1. **One name per concept** (AGI-1.2, DEV-8.2). `stats threshold-events` takes `threshold` (was `level`), the name `stats low-flow-sequences` deploys for the threshold of the same threshold level method, and its result names it `threshold`. `stats duration-curve` takes `exceedance_percents` (was `percents`; the concept of `exceedance_percent`, a percentage carries `_percent`), `bin_method`, `bin_width`, `bin_count` (were `class_*`; `bin_width` is `hydrometrics water-level-zones`' name for the width of a class of a level series); its result has `exceedance_percents`, `curve_exceedance_percents`, `bin_method`, `bin_width`, `bin_count` and `share_percent` in each class and the modal class (was `percent`, the meteo results' word for a share). A run's length in days is `duration_days` everywhere: `meteo spells` has `duration_days`, `max_duration_days`, per year `max_duration_days`, `max_start_date`, `max_end_date` and in the period `n_years` and `mean_max_duration_days` (were `length_days`, `max_length_days`, `longest_*`, `complete_years`, `mean_longest_days`). A list of years is `years` and a list of left-out years `incomplete_years`: `stats seasonality` lists its dated extremes in `years` (was `extremes`), `meteo snow` names `incomplete_years` (was `skipped_years`). A count is `n_*`: `stats baseflow` has `n_values`, `n_spans` and `recession.n_segments` (were `days`, `spans`, `segments`). `meteo spi` gives each month `year` (of `year_type`) and `month` (1 to 12) as integers, as every other result does, instead of a `"YYYY-MM"` string in `month`. Percent options declare the unit `%` as `precip pmaxtp`'s `probability_percent` does.
2. **Variant options** follow `develop`'s pattern (`precip areal`, `precip hietogram`, `hydrograph generate`): an option of one value of `index`, `bin_method` or `threshold_method` has no catalog default, its description says „Only for … ; default …”, the command applies that default, and the option given with another value is `validation_error` `not_applicable`. This covers `meteo precipitation` (`durations_days`, `thresholds_mm`, `wet_day_mm`, `percentiles`), `meteo temperature` (the day thresholds and the degree-day bases), `stats duration-curve` (`bin_width`, `bin_count`) and `stats low-flow-sequences` (`exceedance_percent`; `threshold` with a threshold from the series is now `not_applicable`, was `conflict`).
3. **The batch runs offline and writes only its output.** A line that calls a command that uses the network (`precip pmaxtp`) or gives an output option (`output`, `output_dir`, `schematic`) gets `validation_error` `not_in_batch`, as `commands batch` and `gui serve` do. So the batch's `effects` (`network` false, one `output`) are true, its MCP `openWorldHint` stays false as ADR-0018 requires of every command but `precip pmaxtp`, and its `--check` plan lists every target it writes (DEV-19.10). The envelope keeps AGI-4.2's `items` and ERR §1's `partial` (exit 10) and `failed` (exit 11), checked against Nogat's `result-1` schema.
4. **The reference period of normals.** `meteo precipitation` (`index` `sums`, `heavy`, `seasonality`), `meteo spi` and `meteo temperature` (`index` `means`) take `reference_first_year` and `reference_last_year`, by default the analysed period (`first_year` to `last_year` as resolved), so a call without them gives the numbers it gave before. The analysed years keep their own values; from the reference period, on its own days, come only the baselines: the normals and with them `percent_of_normal`, `anomaly_c`, the classes of relative precipitation and the regime of `seasonality` (WMO-No. 1203, 2017: chapter 2, normals as the benchmark of anomalies; chapter 3 and section 5.1, the climatological standard normal, now 1991–2020, and the 1961–1990 reference normal for climate change; section 4.4.2, a normal needs values in 80 % of the reference period's years), the wet-day percentiles of R95p and R99p (the ETCCDI's base period: Zhang et al., 2011; Klein Tank, Zwiers and Zhang, 2009, WMO-TD No. 1500; WMO-No. 1203 section 5.2.2 allows a period longer than 30 years for percentiles) and the gamma fits of the SPI (WMO-No. 1090, 2012: the SPI compares a total with those of the years on record, at least 30 years; McKee et al., 1993; the Copernicus EDO calibrates on 1991–2020). The result names `reference_first_year`, `reference_last_year` and `reference_period_years`; a reference period other than the analysed one with fewer than 30 years gets its own `short_reference_period`. The other indices take no reference period (`not_applicable`). The annual anomalies of `meteo temperature` against a chosen reference are the input of the climate stripes (M15).
5. **Mann–Kendall with ties.** `stats mann-kendall` corrects Var(S) for groups of equal values, Var(S) = [n(n−1)(2n+5) − Σ t(t−1)(2t+5)]/18 (Kendall, 1975; Hirsch, Slack and Smith, 1982; Gilbert, 1987, Eq. 16.3); Sen's interval is built on it. Every sum of daily precipitation in `meteo` is rounded to 1e-6 mm, as the window sums already were, so sums equal in 0.1 mm are equal numbers and tie. The earlier difference from the mockup (S 46 against 47 at Kraków, −345 against −340 at Praha) came from floating-point sums such as 621.9999999999999 against 622.0, which broke ties; the missing correction itself moved Z only in the sixth digit there. Gilbert's Exercise 16.1 (S 12, Var(S) 43.3, Z 1.67) and Example 16.2's tie groups (Var(S) 1227.33) are reproduced; Exercise 16.3's interval is kept on the uncorrected 44.3, as Gilbert computes it on purpose.
6. **The list of spells.** `meteo spells` takes `min_duration_days` (1 by default): the shortest spell listed in `spells`. The yearly and period summaries always count every spell, because the ETCCDI's CDD and CWD are yearly maximum lengths with no minimum (Zhang et al., 2011) and a distribution of spell lengths needs every spell; the default keeps the list complete, and a long record is shortened by the option or the period.
7. **The GUI**: every option of the 58 commands of „Wszystkie polecenia” has a Polish label, per command where one name means two things in `develop` (`threshold`, `precipitation_mm`), and every command with `one_of` groups names them; a contract test keeps it so. The result view takes a command's own labels first too.
8. **Checked and kept**: `alpha` of `stats baseflow` (the filter's symbol, as `develop` names the Beta shape `alpha`); `precipitation_mm` as a daily series in `meteo` beside the total of `scs effective` (a different concept, labelled apart in the GUI); `meteo`'s column options described with their export defaults (as `route`'s `time_column`); `_percent` in new names where `develop` also has `_pct` (DEV-8.2's form); `harmonics` (the option) beside `n_harmonics` (the result, whose `harmonics` lists the fitted terms); `required` `dates` for a rating curve's period beside `not_applicable` for the period of annual values given without dates (the pairs keep their meaning with dates, annual values do not); `series_year` and `cumulative_year` (two different daily series); the levels of the new notices.

## Consequences

- Visible to users of `develop`'s commands: `stats mann-kendall` on a series with equal values gives a smaller `var_s`, so `z_score` and `p_value` move and `trend_detected` can change near `alpha` (a series without ties is unchanged); `stats low-flow-sequences` computes across gaps (ADR-0029) and refuses `threshold` or `exceedance_percent` of another `threshold_method`, options `develop` did not have. Nothing else of `develop` changes.
- The new result schemas are regenerated; the renamed fields were never released.
- A long record still makes results of tens of kilobytes (Praha's 222 years of spells' summaries alone, about 42,000 characters), beyond DOC-1.7's default page; `first_year`, `last_year` and `min_duration_days` shorten them, and Obserwator runs them through the batch into a file (open question in `docs/PROGRESS.md`).

## Alternatives considered

- **Normals over the analysed years only** (S4): a recent year could not be compared with 1991–2020 nor the SPI calibrated on a baseline, and stripes would change with the years shown.
- **The reference period also for the period's means**: the analysed years would lose their own statistics; only the baselines need a reference.
- **A tie tolerance in `mann_kendall_test`**: an arbitrary tolerance would merge distinct values of other series; exact sums keep exact ties.
- **`min_duration_days` 10 by default, or the list behind an option**: either drops short spells silently from what the command calls its spells, while the summaries already give what the methods need; no default keeps Kraków or Praha within DOC-1.7.
- **Declaring the batch `network: true`**: a command running offline would report an open world for every batch, against ADR-0018's one online command.
