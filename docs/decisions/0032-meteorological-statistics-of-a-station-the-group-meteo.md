# 0032. Meteorological statistics of a station: the group `meteo`, and compact index lines for `tc`, `hydrometrics` and `meteo`

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-13
- Amends: [ADR-0009](0009-compact-index-line-for-large-groups.md) (the groups `tc`, `hydrometrics` and `meteo` get one index line each in `AGENTS.md`, chosen to keep the guide within its budget)
- Builds on: plan `docs/plans/2026-11-13-station-analyses.md` (decision D12 with D1, D2 and D13, step S4), Nogat's plan `2026-11-12-station-data-and-analyses` (step H3: M1 to M14; decisions D8, D12), approved by the author on 2026-11-13; ADR-0029 (the reference period), ADR-0031 (`stats harmonics` and Sen's slope, which serve M12 and M13), ADR-0028 (the providers' lines), ADR-0022

## Context

Obserwator stores the daily series of IMGW-PIB's and ČHMÚ's weather stations (`pl.imgw.meteo_climate_daily`, `pl.imgw.meteo_precip_daily`, `cz.chmi.meteo_daily`: `P` with `P_flag`, `Tmax`, `Tmin`, `Tmean`, `snow_cover`, `fresh_snow`) and shows their statistics; its GUI mockup computed them in a labelled prototype (`mockup/gui/server/analyses/_meteo_proto.py`, Obserwator branch `feat/gui-mockup`). Nogat's D8 asks for the same number in Obserwator and in Hydrolog. D12 of the plan groups the fourteen analyses into six commands with an `index` option where one method serves many indices, and D2 makes the group compact in `AGENTS.md` from its first command.

The guide has a budget of 16,384 bytes (DOC-1.7). After S3 `AGENTS.md` has 16,320 bytes; the `meteo` line with `hydrometrics` made compact, as D2 planned, brings it to 16,588, over the limit. D2 named two ways on: shorter prose of the guide, or a larger budget from Nogat. The `tc` group's seven index lines take 689 bytes.

## Decision

1. **The group `meteo`** (D12, six commands after `hydrometrics`; „Statystyka meteorologiczna” in the GUI). A series is given inline (`dates` and the values) or as `input_file` with `date_column` and a `*_column` option per variable, whose default is the export's name (the unit suffix optional; an optional column with its default name is read when the file has it). `meteo precipitation`, `meteo spells`, `meteo spi` and `meteo temperature` take `year_type` (`calendar`, the default, or `hydrological`, XI–X named after its end) and `first_year`, `last_year` in years of that kind; `meteo snow` counts winters, `meteo hydrothermal` calendar years. As D1, a period keeps the days of its years only; every result names `first_year`, `last_year`, `period_years` and has the notice `short_reference_period` under 30 years.
2. **Missing days by WMO-No. 1203** (2017, section 4.4): a monthly mean or count needs fewer than 11 missing days and fewer than 5 in a row, and a count is scaled to the days of the month; a monthly sum needs every day; monthly extremes take any days; an annual value needs its 12 months; annual maxima, percentile sums, spells and the hydrothermal season need every day of the year; a normal is the mean of the years with a value when they are at least 80 % of the period's years, otherwise `null` with the notice `normal_not_computed` (warning). **Accumulated totals**: a value flagged `accumulated` in `precipitation_flag_column` (by default `<P column>_flag` when the file has it; IMGW-PIB's reading after unread days) stands for the missing days just before it in the monthly sums when, with its own day, they are fewer than 5 and the totals cover fewer than 11 days of the month (4.4.1 c), and is a missing day for every daily statistic; notice `accumulated_totals` (info).
3. **`meteo precipitation`** (M1, M2, M4, M5, M7, M9): `index` `sums` (months, half-years, seasons, years, the normals of the period and each sum in % of its normal), `maxima` (the yearly and monthly largest daily sums and the largest sums of `durations_days`, default 2, 3, 5, 10, with dates; a window counts in the year of its last day, the first window on a tie), `days` (days with P ≥ `thresholds_mm`, default 0.1, 1, 10, 20), `intensity` (SDII of the wet days, P ≥ `wet_day_mm`, 1 mm), `heavy` (R95pTOT and R99pTOT of the `percentiles` 95 and 99 of the wet days of the period, quantile type 8 of Hyndman and Fan, 1996, from at least 100 wet days, otherwise `calculation_error` `too_few_wet_days`) and `seasonality` (the regime of the monthly normals, Oliver's PCI and Walsh and Lawler's SI with their classes, of the normals and of each year). The quantiles of the annual maxima (M3) are `stats flood-frequency` on the values of `maxima`.
4. **`meteo spells`** (M6): `kind` `dry` or `wet`, one per call, and `threshold_mm`, 1 mm by default (the plan and the ETCCDI's CDD and CWD; the mockup's default is 0.1 mm), by the run logic of `stats threshold-events`: a day without a value ends a spell (`open_start`, `open_end`), a spell counts in the year it starts; per complete year the longest and the spells of at least 10, 15 and 20 days, and the distribution of the lengths of the closed spells.
5. **`meteo spi`** (M8): `scales_months` 1 to 48 (1, 3, 6, 12); per calendar month a gamma distribution by Thom's (1958) estimates of the positive k-month sums of the period with the probability of zero (McKee et al., 1993; WMO-No. 1090), fitted when the sums are known in at least 80 % of the years and 3 are positive, otherwise `null` with the notice `spi_not_fitted` (warning); the SPI is held within ±3.09. Each month and year in % of its normal with `precipitation_class`: years in Kaczorowska's (1962) classes, months in the monthly limits (Tomaszewska, 1994, in Gąsiorek and Musiał, 2011, Table 1), because Kaczorowska defined classes of years and seasons only; the mockup applied the annual limits to months, which classes 588 of Kraków's 780 months differently.
6. **`meteo temperature`** (M11): `index` `means` (monthly and annual means of Tmean, Tmax, Tmin, the normal and the anomaly, the extremes with dates), `days` (frost Tmin < 0, ice Tmax < 0, summer Tmax > 25, hot Tmax > 30 °C, tropical nights Tmin > 20 °C), `degree_days` (growing above 5 °C; heating 18 − Tmean on the days with Tmean ≤ 15 °C, Eurostat's rule) and `range` (the mean daily range); every threshold and base is an option with the default above.
7. **`meteo snow`** (M10): a winter is the snow season 1 August to 31 July, named after the hydrological year it ends in. D12 says „per winter (hydrological year)”; the season keeps a whole cold season together, so that snow of late October counts in the winter it begins, and matches the mockup; a literal XI–X year would move 6 of Kraków's 64 winters (among them the earliest first day, 1966-11-01, and the latest last day, 1988-10-31; the medians of the first and last days by 2 days). The days with cover of at least 1 cm (scaled per month), the largest depth with its date, the first and the last day with cover and the fresh snow; a winter needs enough days in October to May, and the partial winters at the ends of a record are listed in `skipped_years`, where the mockup drops them silently.
8. **`meteo hydrothermal`** (M14): Sielianinov's k = P / (0.1 Σt) of each month April to September (Skowera and Puła, 2004) and of the days of each calendar year with Tmean > 10 °C (Selyaninov, 1928), with the nine classes of Skowera and Puła (2004, p. 172) applied to the unrounded k; the mockup's yearly table classed k rounded to two decimals, which classes 6 of Kraków's and 24 of Praha's months differently (the frequencies of the classes are equal).
9. **The period is the data** (D1): a winter half-year or DJF of the first year and an SON of the last that need days outside the period are `null`; the k-month sums of the SPI do not reach before the period, so a period that starts after the record calibrates scales of 3 months and more on fewer sums than the mockup; the default period starts at the first year with any value, where the mockup starts at the first complete year. The mockup's normals of a fixed reference period applied to every year are not computed (open question). Means over years are of unrounded values. Not in D12 and not implemented: Markham's vector, de Martonne's index, McKee's SPI classes and drought events, the days with at least 10 cm of snow, top-10 lists (derivable from `spells` and `years`); the harmonics of temperature (M12) and the trends (M13) are `stats harmonics` and `stats mann-kendall` (ADR-0031).
10. **Notices**: new `normal_not_computed`, `accumulated_totals` and `spi_not_fitted`, with their Polish texts. The English text of `short_reference_period` says „year(s)” instead of „hydrological year(s)”, because the meteorological commands count calendar years; its code, details and Polish text are unchanged.
11. **The providers' lines** (ADR-0028): every `meteo` command takes the option `attribution` (`hydrolog.catalog.groups._attribution.ATTRIBUTION`) and cites it; an Obserwator export read as `input_file` carries its lines (`attribution_source`, `attribution_processed`, `attribution`, `terms_notice`) into the envelope's `attribution` and `terms_notice`.
12. **Budget: three more compact groups** (amends ADR-0009). The groups `hydrometrics` (as D2 planned), `meteo` (from its first command) and `tc` get one index line each in `AGENTS.md`, beside `route` and `stats` (ADR-0009, ADR-0022). Measured: 16,588 bytes with `hydrometrics` and `meteo` compact, 16,224 of 16,384 with `tc` compact too. A compact group is chosen to keep the guide within its budget, not only because it is large; `commands list` and `commands schema` still give each command, and the MCP tools are unchanged. The catalog has 62 commands in 17 groups; the GUI's „Wszystkie polecenia” shows 58 of them.

## Consequences

- Additive: six new result schemas and three new notices; no existing result changes. The English text of `short_reference_period` changes for the `stats` commands too.
- The index of `AGENTS.md` names every command of five groups on one line each; an agent reads the summaries of `tc`, `hydrometrics` and `meteo` with `commands list`. The deviation from DOC-4.2 in `CLAUDE.md` names the five groups.
- The mockup's numbers on Kraków (250190390) and Praha-Klementinum (0-203-0-11514) are reproduced to its rounding (the plan's Results), with the differences of items 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9.
- `meteo spells` lists every spell: Kraków's dry spells at 1 mm are 3,859, about 460 KB of JSON.
- The methods with their sources are in `docs/COMPUTATION_PATHS.md`, section 3.13.

## Alternatives considered

- **Shorter prose of the guide** (D2; the third typical flow and the install line, about 250 bytes): it removes a worked flow that an agent cannot get from `commands list`, to keep index lines that `commands list` gives anyway, and leaves the next command no room.
- **A larger budget from Nogat** (DOC-1.7): a change of the standard for every member, for one tool's index, waiting on Nogat's review, while Hydrolog can stay within the budget by its own means.
- **One command per analysis** (M1 to M14): the index and the MCP tool list would grow by fourteen entries where one method serves several indices (D2).
- **The hydrological year XI–X as the winter of `meteo snow`**: it cuts the cold season at 1 November and moves early snow into the winter before.
- **Kaczorowska's annual limits for months**, as the mockup: her classes are defined for years and seasons, and limits for months are published (Gąsiorek and Musiał, 2011, Table 1).
- **A dry-day threshold of 0.1 mm** by default, as the mockup: the plan and the ETCCDI's CDD and CWD use 1 mm; 0.1 mm is a value of `threshold_mm`.
- **A reference period for normals beside the period of the data** (the mockup's anomalies against 1991–2020 in every year, the SPI calibrated on 1991–2020 for the whole record): D12 has no such option and D1 makes the chosen period the data; left as an open question for the author.
